Proving Your Docs Work Without Guessing

Page views are vanity. They tell you that someone visited — not that anyone understood, completed a task, or walked away more capable than when they arrived.

Documentation teams that measure only page views are running a newspaper model in a product world. The right metrics don't just tell you what happened. They tell you whether your documentation is doing its job.

The Five Metrics That Actually Matter​

1. Ticket Deflection Rate​

The percentage of users who resolved their issue through documentation without contacting support. This is your headline number — the one that translates directly into business value and speaks loudest to leadership.

How to measure it: Compare support ticket volume before and after a documentation improvement initiative. Some teams track it through self-service rates in their support portal.

2. Search Success Rate vs. Search Failure Rate​

When a user searches your documentation, do they find something useful — or do they hit a dead end and leave?

Search failure rate is the percentage of searches that return no results or result in the user immediately leaving the site. A high failure rate means your content doesn't match the language your users actually use.

How to act on it: Export your zero-result search queries monthly. Each one is a topic you haven't covered — or a topic you've covered under a name no one searches for.

3. Time-to-Task Completion​

How long does it take a user to complete a task using your documentation? A well-structured article gets a user from question to answer in under two minutes. An article that requires the user to read three other articles first, backtrack, and search again is failing — regardless of how accurate it is.

How to measure it: Session recording tools and usability testing give you this data. At the team level, structured content reviews where someone unfamiliar with the product attempts a task using only the documentation are surprisingly revealing.

4. Article Feedback Score​

The simple thumbs-up/thumbs-down at the bottom of every article is more valuable than most teams treat it. It is a continuous, low-friction signal from the exact user who just tried to use your content.

What to look for: Articles with consistently low scores despite high traffic are your highest-priority rewrites. Articles with high scores but low traffic may need better discoverability, not better content.

5. Content Findability Score​

Can users navigate to the information they need? Findability measures whether your information architecture — your categories, labels, navigation, and search — actually leads users to the right place.

How to measure it: Track the path users take before reaching a high-value article. If most users arrive via search rather than navigation, your information architecture may be working against discoverability.

Building a Reporting Dashboard That Leadership Understands​

The documentation team at SAP Business Network tracked five key metrics across the SAP Help Portal: article views, search success and failure rates, deflection rates, user feedback scores, and time on page. These were surfaced monthly in Power BI dashboards and presented to leadership as content performance indicators — not vanity stats.

The shift this created was significant. Documentation moved from being evaluated on output (how many articles were published) to being evaluated on outcome (how effectively those articles served users). That distinction changes the conversation entirely.

When you can walk into a quarterly review and say "our updated invoice troubleshooting guide deflected 340 tickets last quarter, which at an average handle time of 12 minutes represents approximately 68 hours of support effort saved" — you are no longer reporting on documentation. You are reporting on business impact.

Where to Start if You Have No Data Yet​

If your team has no measurement infrastructure, start with two things:

Google Analytics on your help portal — set it up this week if it isn't already running. It gives you traffic, search queries, bounce rates, and session duration at zero cost. Every insight you need to begin making data-driven improvements is available here.

A simple article feedback widget — a thumbs-up/down at the bottom of every article, feeding into a spreadsheet or your analytics platform. Three months of feedback data will reveal your ten most problematic articles faster than any content audit.

From there, build upward. The goal is not a perfect dashboard on day one. The goal is to replace the question "do you think this article is working?" with "here is what the data shows."

That shift — from instinct to evidence — is what moves documentation from a support function to a strategic one.
