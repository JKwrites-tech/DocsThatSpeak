# Documentation Strategy

Most of us have been handed a completed feature and asked to "just document it."

This section is about changing that. It covers how to measure whether your work is actually landing, how to build feedback loops that keep your content accurate, and how to show what documentation is doing for the business.

Because the writers who get a seat at the product table are not the ones who deliver the most pages. They are the ones who can prove their work matters.

---

## What you will find here

### Measure impact

Your docs are doing more than answering questions. They are keeping users out of the support queue, helping new users get productive faster, and driving product adoption. This section shows you how to connect that work to numbers your stakeholders actually care about.

### Build feedback loops

Documentation does not stay accurate on its own. Every product update, renamed feature, or changed workflow is a potential gap waiting to happen. This section covers how to set up a system that catches those gaps early, so you are not discovering outdated content because a user complained about it.

### Position strategically

Most writers get resourced when they can show impact. This section is about learning to speak the language of the business, so documentation stops being treated as overhead and starts being treated as something worth investing in.

---

## Why strategy matters

The writers who get invited into product conversations early are not always the most experienced ones. They can show how many support tickets their docs deflected, how quickly new users found their footing, and whether the content is actually being used.

That is what strategy gives you. A way to define what good looks like, build processes that keep your content accurate across releases, and talk about your work in terms that resonate with the people who make decisions.
