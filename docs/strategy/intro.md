# Documentation Strategy

> Documentation is not a deliverable. It is a function.

When documentation works, it reduces support costs, accelerates onboarding, and earns a seat at the product table. This section covers the strategic side of technical writing: how to measure impact, build feedback loops, and position documentation as a business driver rather than an afterthought.

---

## What this section covers

### Measure impact

Connect documentation to metrics that matter to the business. Good documentation is not invisible effort; it is a traceable lever on support ticket volume, time-to-productivity for new users, and product adoption rates.

### Build feedback loops

Create systems that keep documentation accurate, relevant, and continuously improving. Without structured feedback, even well-written docs drift out of sync with the product they describe.

### Position strategically

Frame documentation as infrastructure, not overhead, and advocate for it accordingly. Writers who understand business outcomes are writers who get resourced.

---

## Why strategy matters

Most documentation problems are not writing problems. They are prioritization problems, ownership problems, or measurement problems. A technically skilled writer without a strategic foundation will consistently find themselves reactive: updating docs after releases, chasing SMEs for reviews, and justifying headcount with anecdotes instead of data.

This section exists to change that. The goal is to help you operate proactively: define what good looks like, build the processes that sustain it, and communicate value in terms your stakeholders already care about.

:::tip Before you continue
The frameworks in this section apply whether you are a solo writer on a startup team or part of a large documentation organization. Scale the approach to your context, not the other way around.
:::
